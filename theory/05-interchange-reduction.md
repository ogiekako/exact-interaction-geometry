# Interchange: scalar collapse and typed decomposition loss

## 0. Status and purpose

This note performs the smallest PROP/interchange stress test proposed by the beyond-Core
programme. It reaches a negative calibration rather than a new extraction theorem:

- in the doubly unital untyped sector, strict interchange is the classical Eckmann--Hilton
  hypothesis and collapses to the commutative-monoid case already tested;
- in a typed strict monoidal category, interchange equates row-first and column-first syntax, so
  a chosen hierarchical root decomposition does not descend;
- the full typed PROP geometry is not collapsed by Eckmann--Hilton, because tensor is not an
  internal second multiplication on a general Hom set.

All positive ingredients are standard. The project-specific value is the exact reduction and
the input/output audit.

## 1. The one-type strict test is Eckmann--Hilton

Let `X` carry two binary operations, written `circle` and `tensor`, each with a two-sided unit
`e_circle` and `e_tensor`. Assume strict interchange:

```text
(a tensor b) circle (c tensor d)
  = (a circle c) tensor (b circle d).                  (1.1)
```

### Theorem 1.1 — strict interchange collapse **[PROVED / CLASSICAL]**

The two units coincide, the two operations coincide, and the resulting operation is associative
and commutative.

### Proof

Substitute `(a,b,c,d)=(e_circle,e_tensor,e_tensor,e_circle)` in (1.1). The left side reduces to
`e_circle` using the `tensor` unit, while the right side reduces to `e_tensor` using the `circle`
unit. Write their common value as `e`.

Then

```text
a circle b
  = (a tensor e) circle (e tensor b)
  = (a circle e) tensor (e circle b)
  = a tensor b,
```

so the operations agree. A second use of interchange gives

```text
a circle b
  = (e tensor a) circle (b tensor e)
  = (e circle b) tensor (a circle e)
  = b tensor a.
```

Thus the common operation is commutative. It is also associative even if this was not assumed at
the outset. Writing the common operation by juxtaposition, interchange and the unit give

```text
(ab)c = (ab)(ec) = (ae)(bc) = a(bc).
```

Thus the output is a commutative monoid. QED.

The unital hypotheses are real. If `tensor` is the left-projection operation
`a tensor b=a`, then (1.1) holds with `circle` equal to **any** monoid operation:

```text
(a tensor b) circle (c tensor d)
  = a circle c
  = (a circle c) tensor (b circle d).
```

The projection operation has no two-sided unit on a non-singleton set, and `circle` may be
noncommutative. Hence interchange without the second unit does not imply the collapse.

## 2. Exact PROP scope: only the scalar sector collapses

Let `C` be a strict monoidal category with tensor unit `I`. On the scalar set `C(I,I)`, vertical
composition and tensor are two unital operations with common unit `id_I`; bifunctoriality of
tensor is interchange. Theorem 1.1 therefore gives the standard result

```text
End_C(I) is a commutative monoid.
```

For a PROP, whose objects are natural numbers under addition, this applies to `End(0)`.

It does **not** apply to a general `End(n)`: tensor sends

```text
End(n) x End(n) -> End(2n),
```

so it is not a second internal multiplication on `End(n)` unless `n=0`. Nor does it identify
composition and tensor on all morphisms, whose source and target types differ. Thus a one-object
or unit-endomorphism calibration cannot stand in for the typed PROP problem.

This scope distinction is witnessed inside PROPs. In the free PROP on two unary generators
`x,y:1->1`, strict interchange holds, while `x circle y` and `y circle x` are distinct elements
of `End(1)`. Interchange therefore does not make general unary operations commute.

## 3. The first typed grid loses a chosen hierarchy

Consider the free PROP generated by four unary operations

```text
a,b,c,d : 1 -> 1.
```

Bifunctoriality gives the equality in `P(2,2)`

```text
(a tensor b) circle (c tensor d)
  = (a circle c) tensor (b circle d).                  (3.1)
```

The left syntax tree has a serial/composition root whose children are parallel composites. The
right syntax tree has a parallel/tensor root whose children are serial composites.

### Proposition 3.1 — root hierarchy does not descend **[PROVED / DIRECT]**

The function on raw binary syntax that records whether the root constructor is `circle` or
`tensor` does not factor through the strict-interchange quotient.

### Proof

The two sides of (3.1) represent the same quotient morphism, while their root labels are
different. QED.

This does not say that the quotient morphism has no decomposition geometry. It has both the
displayed vertical factorization and the displayed tensor factorization. What fails is the
attempt to retain one representative's top-level hierarchy as a literal point-valued quotient
invariant.

In fact, the flat labelled `2 x 2` incidence grid survives: `a,c` lie on one wire and `b,d` on the
other in both presentations, with the same within-wire composition order. Interchange forgets
the row-first versus column-first **scheduling hierarchy**, not the underlying grid. A claim that
all two-dimensional geometry is destroyed would therefore be false.

As in the word case, two extremal repairs are immediately available once a presentation is
fixed:

- keep only decompositions shared by every representative;
- saturate by all decompositions occurring in some representative.

Interchange makes the second repair contain both row-first and column-first layouts. Selecting a
higher cell witnessing their comparison, rather than equating them, is a different truncation-
level problem and requires proof-relevant interchange/coherence input.

## 4. Relation to the earlier quotient boundaries

The two parts of this test do not yield a new independent generic extractor.

1. In the untyped doubly unital sector, Theorem 1.1 reduces the semantics to a commutative
   monoid. The cut-descent and isotropy obstructions of the preceding two notes apply.
2. In the typed sector, Proposition 3.1 is the two-constructor analogue of representative-cut
   failure: an equation relates terms exposing incompatible decompositions at their roots.
3. If interchange is retained as an invertible witness rather than imposed as equality, its
   automorphisms and relations disappear under component truncation exactly as permutation
   isotropy did.

The smallest test therefore supports the current quotient-first diagnosis but does not yet
separate a specifically two-dimensional invariant. A genuinely new result must use typed
multi-boundary structure or nontrivial coherence in a way not reducible to commutative collapse,
syntax saturation, or 0-truncation.

## 5. Input/output audit

### Supplied in the untyped theorem

- two total binary operations on the same set;
- two-sided units for both operations;
- the full four-variable interchange equation.

### Supplied in the typed grid

- a typed strict monoidal category (or PROP sector);
- both composition and tensor, including their typing;
- bifunctoriality/interchange as an equality;
- the raw serial/parallel syntax whose hierarchy is later tested.

### Actually forced

- **untyped:** equality and commutativity of the two operations (the classical
  Eckmann--Hilton conclusion);
- **typed:** failure of a representative-selected root label to descend; the quotient morphism
  admits both displayed factorizations.

### Not forced

- a canonical choice between row-first and column-first hierarchy;
- proof-relevant interchange witnesses or their higher coherence;
- a PROP arity category, graphical nerve, Segal recognition theorem, or algebra classifier;
- any claim that strict interchange itself was recovered from weaker observations.

### Anti-tautology verdict

Strict interchange has genuine algebraic force, but it is already a strong supplied law. Its
one-type consequence is classical commutativity, and the typed syntax example only exposes the
same descent-policy choice found for word quotients. The smallest PROP test is therefore a
**PRIOR-ART REDUCTION / NEGATIVE CALIBRATION**, not evidence for a new EIG geometry.

## 6. Prior-art boundary

The collapse in Theorem 1.1 is the
[Eckmann--Hilton argument](https://eudml.org/doc/160908). Modern categorical formulations include
Batanin, [*The Eckmann--Hilton argument and higher operads*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0207281),
and the scalar application is discussed by Suárez-Álvarez,
[*The Hilton--Eckmann argument for cup-products*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0209029). A standard
PROP definition with objects `N` and tensor given by addition appears in Pirashvili,
[*On the PROP corresponding to bialgebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0110014).

The commutativity of scalar endomorphisms of a monoidal unit and strict
interchange/bifunctoriality are standard categorical facts. No novelty is claimed for them.
The theorem here requires total operations, two-sided units, and strict interchange on one
carrier. Partial composition/local units, transported convolution products, and weak or
homotopy-coherent interchange require separate hypotheses; higher-categorical versions need not
yield literal commutativity. Genuinely PROP-specific coherence phenomena remain outside the
proved result.
